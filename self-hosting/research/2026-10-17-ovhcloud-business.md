# OVHcloud as a long-term home for the mail and media servers (2026-10-17)

**Verdict: adequate.** OVHcloud is not a company in distress. It earns a 40% adjusted EBITDA margin, holds leverage at 2.6x with a stated 3x ceiling, has no debt due before about 2029, and its US arm passed €100M of revenue and is still growing. But it is not a strong, cash-rich business either. Net income is near zero, capex eats most of the operating cash, growth fell short of its own 2024 plan, and the founder family controls about 79% and changes the top team often. For the household, the larger risks sit at the account level, not the company level. The US terms let OVH suspend without notice, destroy data on termination, and cap liability at twelve months of fees. RAM-driven price rises are running through 2026. The US VPS API is labeled BETA end to end. OVH is a reasonable place to rent two cheap servers, as long as the design assumes the account can vanish on any given day. It is not a place to trust with the only copy of anything.

The FY2026 annual results come out on 20 October 2026, three days after this brief ([financial calendar](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/investor-relations/financial-results/)). The figures below run to the Q3 release of 25 June 2026. Re-read the brief against the full-year release before ordering.

Unlevered free cash flow was €32.3M and net income €5.9M. ## 2. Strategy and growth

Public Cloud is the engine. VPS now sits inside that segment, and new VPS customers rose 116% in H1 after the entry range was repositioned ([H1 FY26](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news/financial-results-h1-fy26/)). The Q3 release credits the VPS 2027 range launched on 17 June 2026 ([OVHcloud blog](https://blog.ovhcloud.com/vps-2027/)). In other words, the cheap VPS is a product OVH is pushing, not one it is winding down. Existing VPS plans keep their configuration and price, with no forced migration.

That came after a sharp rise. In March 2026 OVH raised the 2025-line VPS prices by 43% to 49% from April, blaming RAM. Existing customers could lock current rates for up to two years by renewing early ([OVHcloud blog, Mar 2026](https://blog.ovhcloud.com/en/posts/pricing-evolution-of-public-cloud-bare-metal-and-vps-at-ovhcloud/)). Dedicated servers rose by up to 87% from August 2026 ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ovhcloud-to-raise-dedicated-server-prices-by-up-to-87-percent-from-september/)). On the Q3 call, management said RAM cost six times as much as a year before and that more increases were being prepared. A French report says OVH considers further increases in 2027 "very likely" ([JustGeek via search](https://www.justgeek.fr/?p=156725), unverified).

Sovereignty demand is real but European. A consortium with OVH won an EU institutions cloud contract capped at €180M over six years ([Q3 release](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/sites/default/files/external_files/2026-06-25-ovhcloud-q3-fy26-pr-en-vdef.pdf)). None of that helps a US VPS customer directly.

**The US business is growing, not shrinking.** The contracting entity is OVH US LLC, a Delaware company based in Reston, Virginia ([US Terms of Service](https://us.ovhcloud.com/legal/terms-of-service/)). It was formed on 25 August 2016 ([OVHcloud US, Aug 2026](https://us.ovhcloud.com/press/press-releases/2026/ovhcloud-us-celebrates-ten-years-of-growth-and-innovation/)). US revenue passed €100M in FY2025, compounding above 20% a year since FY2023 ([FY25 release](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news/financial-results-fy25/)). Rest of World grew 14.3% in FY2025, 9.5% in H1, and 8.6% in Q3, with US Public Cloud named as the driver. Vint Hill opened in 2017 and Hillsboro in 2018. Hillsboro is listed at about 4 MW ([datacenter.fyi](https://www.datacenter.fyi/company/ovhcloud), third party). A Seattle Local Zone opened in March 2025 ([OVHcloud US](https://us.ovhcloud.com/press/press-releases/2025/ovhcloud-us-expands-cloud-network-with-new-seattle-local-zone/)). I found no US layoffs, closures, or sale talk. One older Journal du Net piece says the US weighs on margins, but I could not read or date its text.

The US entity is legally separate from the French group by design. OVH says US data can be reached under the CLOUD Act ([OVHcloud US CLOUD Act FAQ](https://us.ovhcloud.com/legal/faqs/cloud-act/)). One practical consequence: a 2020 customer reported that EU phone support told him the US site is "a different company" and could not help ([WebHostingTalk, Mar 2020](https://www.webhostingtalk.com/showthread.php?t=1800892), anecdote).

## 3. Operational track record

**The 2021 Strasbourg fire.** On 10 March 2021, SBG2 burned down and four of SBG1's twelve rooms were destroyed. The French investigators (BEA-RI) did not determine the cause. They noted an electrical fault around a UPS and possible moisture at an inverter. The fire service found no automatic extinguishing system and no general power cut-off ([The Register, Jun 2022](https://www.theregister.com/2022/06/10/ovhcloud_datacenter_fire_last_year/)). The Lille commercial court ordered OVH to pay Bati Courtage €101,102 (3 February 2023) and Bluepad €153,837 (16 March 2023). In both cases OVH had sold a backup service and then kept the copies in the same building, or purged them. The court rejected OVH's force majeure defence ([Blocks & Files, 23 Mar 2023](https://www.blocksandfiles.com/data-protection/2023/03/23/ovhcloud-must-pay-damages-for-lost-backup-data/1594496), [Le Monde Informatique](https://www.lemondeinformatique.fr/actualites/lireamp-incendie-sgb2-strasbourg-ovh-condamne-a-verser-plus-de-100-000-eteuro-89434.html)). The awards were a fraction of what was claimed. OVH appealed, and I found no appeal ruling. A class action by about 140 customers has no reported outcome. The lesson is narrow: OVH was liable for breaking a backup promise it had sold, not for losing data the customer never backed up. Afterwards OVH promised free backups for everyone and a "Hyper Resilience" plan ([The Register, May 2021](https://www.theregister.com/2021/05/06/ovh_outlines_threepoint_hyper_resiliance/)). I found no audit of what changed in its buildings.

**Other outages.** On 13 October 2021, a human error during router work at Vint Hill took down OVH's global backbone for about an hour ([OVHcloud](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news/network-incident/)). Aggregators list a run of US backbone and Local Zone incidents from mid-July 2025, a Hillsboro network outage on 20 February 2026, and fibre cuts raising BHS-to-HIL latency on 15 July 2026 ([StatusGator](https://statusgator.com/services/ovhcloud/outage-history)). These are third-party records with date gaps. On 9 July 2026 an emergency security patch cut off some EU VPSes for hours, with no ticket reply in the thread ([OVH community](https://community.ovhcloud.com/t/urgent-multiple-vps-servers-down-total-network-outage-lockout-after-sudden-shutdown/53516), anecdote).

**Status page.** The US status page runs on a hosted status service, with per-site VPS components and email, webhook, and RSS subscriptions ([status.us.ovhcloud.com](https://status.us.ovhcloud.com/)). It currently announces three planned four-hour host power-offs in Hillsboro on 12, 17, and 18 November 2026, each covering VPS. Mail should plan on some windows like that every year. Sending servers queue and retry for days, so four hours is harmless to inbound mail if DNS and the relay are healthy.

## 4. Customer experience risk for a small customer

**Terms.** The US Terms of Service took effect on 15 May 2025 ([US ToS](https://us.ovhcloud.com/legal/terms-of-service/)):

- OVH may suspend "without prior notice" if it finds a breach or a risk of harm (§10(a)).
- Failing identity or payment verification leads to "immediate termination" (§2(b)).
- After termination OVH "WILL DESTROY ALL CONTENT DATA." A 15-day retrieval extension is discretionary and only for compliant customers (§9(e), §9(f)).
- Lost data is excluded from damages, and liability is capped at fees paid in the prior twelve months (§13(a)). For this household that is under $200.
- Prices can rise on 30 days' notice, effective at the next renewal (§3(d)).
- Disputes go to Fairfax County, Virginia, with a jury waiver (§19(c)).

The VPS terms put backups solely on the customer. The IP addresses remain OVH property and are non-transferable ([Service-Specific Terms](https://us.ovhcloud.com/legal/service-specific-terms/)).

**Suspension stories.** The clearest US termination story I found dates from 2020 and involved a law-enforcement report about hosted content. It ended with a "final" termination and no data access ([WebHostingTalk](https://www.webhostingtalk.com/showthread.php?t=1800892), anecdote). I found no 2025-2026 pattern of US VPS accounts terminated without cause. On Trustpilot, OVHcloud US scores 3.7 from 311 reviews. Two 2026 reviews complain about identity checks that asked for passport, selfie, and card photos. Others report a server that never booted, a "98% price increase with 1 month notice," and tickets closed without a reply. Many five-star reviews praise named support staff ([Trustpilot, Jun-Oct 2026](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/us.ovhcloud.com), anecdote).

**Support.** The free Standard level is described as "support for non-critical environments." The page sets no response target for it ([OVHcloud US support levels](https://us.ovhcloud.com/support-levels/)). Expect tickets only, and expect slowness.

**Mail abuse handling.** Outbound spam is caught automatically by Vade Secure. The first block on port 25 lasts an hour, and repeats last longer. The customer can unblock through the panel or the API at `/ip/{ip}/spam/.../unblock` ([AntiSpam guide](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/bare-metal-cloud/dedicated-servers/antispam-best-practices)). Third-party complaints are checked for admissibility and forwarded to the customer. Sanctions range "from a warning to full termination," and phishing can trigger automatic suspension by complaint volume ([OVH abuse procedures](https://www.ovh.com/ma/abuse/documents/processing_procedures_forwarded_to_the_abuse_department.pdf)). I found no published response deadline. For this household the realistic trigger is a compromised mailbox password used to send spam. Stalwart's outbound path through the relay lowers the network-level exposure, but abuse reports would still reach OVH.

## 5. Comparison with Hetzner

Hetzner is privately held by its founders' circle and files only statutory German accounts. The last widely cited figures are €367M revenue and €103M net income for 2021 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetzner)). A November 2025 regional report describes profit in the "three-digit millions," while its headline says half a billion ([Nürnberger Nachrichten](https://www.nn.de/region/weissenburg/halbe-milliarde-gewinn-weissenburg-gunzenhausen-profitiert-mit-hetzner-online-vom-it-boom-rep2-1.14910656)). Those two do not agree, and I could not verify recent figures. On the evidence, Hetzner is far more profitable relative to its size, carries no public debt, and has no market or founder-succession drama. OVH is bigger, publicly audited, leveraged, and still in heavy capex.

Neither provider looks likely to fail within five years. Over a decade, both carry the same main risk for a household: an account decision made by staff with little appeal. Hetzner has its own record of abrupt account restrictions and terminations, which it says are always manually reviewed ([LowEndTalk](https://lowendtalk.com/discussion/198162/hetzner-is-cancelling-all-of-our-servers-without-reason), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/hetzner.com?page=6), anecdote). Both raised prices in 2026 over RAM ([CDNsun](https://blog.cdnsun.com/ovhcloud-and-hetzner-2026-hosting-price-increases-explained/)). On durability, Hetzner wins narrowly. On stability for this household, they are about even. On latency and price for US-West, OVH wins clearly (see the [hosting brief](2026-10-17-hosting.md)).

## 6. API and automation

**Auth model.** The classic API uses an application key and secret plus a consumer key. Each consumer key carries `rules` (HTTP method plus path, with `*` wildcards), an optional `allowedIPs` list, and an `expiration`. All of these are PRODUCTION in the US schema at `api.us.ovhcloud.com/1.0/auth.json`, which I read on 2026-10-17. Credentials can be listed, edited, and deleted through `/me/api/credential`, and deleting an application revokes every credential under it. The rules are allow-only. So a key scoped as `/vps/<name>/*` also allows `POST .../terminate`. A safe key lists routes one by one. OVH's Terraform team admits that order and termination flows need routes outside the product's own paths. One user called the resulting broad tokens "a big security hole" ([terraform-provider-ovh #1448, Sep 2026](https://github.com/ovh/terraform-provider-ovh/issues/1448)).

The newer model is OAuth2 service accounts governed by IAM policies. Those policies name resources by URN, can target a single VPS, and support excepted actions ([IAM policy guide](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/account-and-service-management/account-information/iam-policy-ui.md), [service accounts](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/manage-and-operate/api/manage-service-account)). In the US, IAM policy endpoints (`/v2/iam/policy`) are PRODUCTION, but creating an OAuth2 client (`/me/api/oauth2/client`) is BETA. The Go and Python SDKs know the US token URL `us.ovhcloud.com/auth/oauth2/token` ([go-ovh](https://github.com/ovh/go-ovh), [python-ovh](https://github.com/ovh/python-ovh)). The IAM guide says ordering and billing actions are "not yet available" in IAM.

**Audit logs.** Account audit, activity (API and panel actions), and access-policy logs can be forwarded to a Logs Data Platform stream. Forwarding is free, but the LDP stream is billed ([logs forwarding guide](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/manage-and-operate/iam/logs-forwarding)). In the US schema, the subscription endpoints are PRODUCTION and a direct `GET /me/logs/audit` is ALPHA. Each credential also shows `lastUse`.

**VPS coverage.** Every one of the 56 non-deprecated `/vps` operations in the US schema is marked BETA, down to `GET /vps`. The EU schema marks 63 of the same tree PRODUCTION and only 4 BETA, `rebuild` among them. The US tree covers reboot, start and stop, `rebuild` (with `imageId`, `sshKey`, `doNotSendPassword`, and a first-boot `postInstallScript`), one snapshot with revert and download, automated-backup restore, console URL, terminate, and datacenter stock checks. Reverse DNS (`/ip/{ip}/reverse`), the edge firewall, and the anti-spam unblock live under `/ip` and are PRODUCTION. Ordering goes through `/order` carts. I found no documented rate limit for the v1 API. Public Cloud's OpenStack APIs allow 20 requests per second ([rate limits](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/public-cloud/cross-functional/api-rate-limits)).

**Tooling.**

- The official `ovhcloud` CLI is a year old (beta launch September 2025) and at v0.15.0, with 64 open issues. A reinstall with `--do-not-send-password` left `authorized_keys` empty until a fix in August 2026 ([ovhcloud-cli](https://github.com/ovh/ovhcloud-cli)).
- The Terraform provider is active (v2.22.0, 2 October 2026). Its `ovh_vps` resource covers about 15% of the VPS API. A breaking rework is pending ([#1126](https://github.com/ovh/terraform-provider-ovh/issues/1126)), and an open bug says VPS creation with SSH keys is hard to automate ([#1229](https://github.com/ovh/terraform-provider-ovh/issues/1229)). A community PR for 99% coverage, tested against the US API, is still unmerged ([#1322](https://github.com/ovh/terraform-provider-ovh/issues/1322)).
- python-ovh's last release was in August 2024, and go-ovh's in June 2025.

Two dated breakages: the VPS `reinstall` route was deprecated in favour of the BETA `rebuild`, and dedicated-server personal templates were retired on 7 October 2025 ([OVHcloud US](https://support.us.ovhcloud.com/hc/en-us/articles/40842755153171-End-of-life-for-personal-installation-templates)).

**Hetzner, by contrast.** A Hetzner Cloud token belongs to one project and is either Read (GET only) or Read & Write ([Hetzner docs](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/api/getting-started/generating-api-token/)). It has no per-server scope, no IP allow-list, and no expiry. But the Cloud API exposes no billing or account endpoints (my reading of its reference), so a project token cannot buy, pay, or close the account. The rate limit is reported as 3,600 requests an hour ([Jentic](https://jentic.com/apis/hetzner-cloud), third party). `hcloud` is at v1.70.1, with 1,843 stars and 26 open issues ([hetznercloud/cli](https://github.com/hetznercloud/cli)). Hetzner's API is simpler and more mature. OVH's permission model is finer but harder to get right.

**Verdict on Claude managing OVH.** Claude can run day-to-day work safely: status reads, reboots, rDNS, firewall rules, snapshots, and rebuilds from the repo. The prerequisite is a key built for that job. The API is good enough, not top-notch. The US VPS tree is BETA, the CLI is pre-1.0, Terraform barely covers VPS, and rate limits are undocumented. So scripts should call the API through the official SDK or CLI, poll task IDs, and treat any schema change as a reason to stop. Ordering, renewals, and termination stay with Geoff in the panel.

Recommended scope: one consumer key with explicit rules only.

- `GET` on `/vps/<mail>/*` and `/vps/<media>/*`.
- `POST` on those two VPSes' `reboot`, `start`, `stop`, `rebuild`, `createSnapshot`, `snapshot/revert`, `getConsoleUrl`, and `automatedBackup/restore`, plus `DELETE` on their `snapshot`.
- `GET`, `POST`, `PUT`, and `DELETE` on `/ip/<ip>/reverse*` and `/ip/<ip>/firewall*` for the four addresses, and `GET` on `/ip/<ip>/spam*`.
- No `/me`, `/order`, `/services`, `terminate`, or `confirmTermination`.

Add `allowedIPs` if pavilion's address is stable, set an expiration of a few months, and store the key in the age store. Keep a second, `GET`-only key for monitoring. Claude should never hold the account password or second factor. Move to an IAM service account once OAuth2 clients leave BETA in the US.

## Risks

- **Account loss is the dominant risk.** Suspension can come without notice, data is destroyed on termination, and the IPs are not portable. Both servers sit in one account, so one decision takes mail and media together.
- **Mail abuse.** One compromised mailbox can trigger port 25 blocks or abuse escalation. Sanctions run up to termination, and no response deadline is published.
- **Price drift.** RAM costs are still rising. VPS prices rose 43% to 49% in April 2026, and further 2027 rises are expected. US terms allow increases on 30 days' notice at renewal.
- **Thin cash generation.** Free cash flow is small against €1.1bn of debt. A capex or rate shock would squeeze the company, though nothing falls due before about 2029.
- **Governance.** A family holds about 79%, there were two surprise C-suite exits in a year, and the company missed its 2024 plan. Strategy can turn quickly.
- **Automation fragility.** The US VPS API is all BETA and the CLI is pre-1.0, so provisioning scripts may break without notice.
- **Planned maintenance.** Four-hour host power-offs happen, with three announced for Hillsboro in November 2026.

## Mitigations

The design already has the core of this: the home USB backup, git-provisioned servers, and DNS on Cloudflare. Cheap additions:

1. **Break the recovery loop.** The OVH, Cloudflare, registrar, and relay accounts must log in and recover through an address not hosted on Stalwart, with second-factor backup codes stored offline. If mail is down, those accounts are how you rebuild it.
2. **Pre-verify a standby provider.** Pass KYC at a second provider now, and keep a $0 or $5 standing account, so a rebuild never waits on an 11-day document check. Hetzner already has an account.
3. **Rehearse a cold rebuild there once a year** from git plus the restic copy. Time it, and record the steps in the runbook.
4. **Add a second off-site copy** of the mail store beyond the USB disk, for example restic to Cloudflare R2. That covers a lost house disk or a long absence.
5. **Keep DNS ready to move.** Use TTLs of 300 seconds on the MX and A/AAAA records. Keep SPF and DKIM provider-neutral, sending through the relay, so a new IP needs only one or two records changed.
6. **Watch OVH from outside.** Subscribe to the Hillsboro VPS components on the status page by email or RSS. Watch the account's notification email, which must not be on Stalwart.
7. **Harden against abuse triggers.** Use strong unique passwords and app passwords per device, Stalwart rate limits on authenticated sending, and an alert on outbound volume.
8. **Hold the price.** Once the rehearsal passes, take the twelve-month term. Renew early if a rise is announced, since early renewal locked old rates in March 2026.
9. **Scope the API key** as in section 6, with no billing or termination routes.
10. **Optional: split accounts,** with mail at a second provider, as the hosting brief's runner-up describes. It is the only mitigation that stops one account action from taking down both services.
