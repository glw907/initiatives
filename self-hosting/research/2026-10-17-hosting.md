# Hosting provider choice for the mail and media workloads (2026-10-17)

This brief compares hosting providers on equal footing for the self-hosting initiative. Hetzner is one option among the others, not the default. It prices two server shapes at every provider, as orderable today. The one-server shape is a single 8 GB server plus a block volume of about 20 GB for mail data. The two-server shape is a small 2 to 4 GB mail server plus a 4 to 8 GB media server. Every service runs in Docker Compose, with Stalwart on host networking.

The brief builds on the [server baseline brief](2026-10-17-server-baseline.md) and the [mail cutover brief](2026-10-17-mail-cutover.md). The mail brief settled on sending outbound mail through a relay on port 587, so outbound port 25 and the provider's IP reputation matter less than they would for direct sending. Inbound port 25, PTR control, and account stability still matter fully.

The owner's weighting is low cost, then stability, then performance. Stability means reliable uptime and no account-termination or noisy-neighbor risk. Performance means real CPU and disk for Immich ML and thumbnails, plus steady network for music. Mail-friendliness is a hard requirement wherever Stalwart runs.

## Workload and sizing

Stalwart serves 4 to 5 users. Stalwart's docs say 1 GB of RAM and one core suit 5 to 10 users, and one operator reported a 619 MB peak over two months (both cited in the [Stalwart brief](2026-10-17-stalwart.md)). The mailbox size on disk is still unmeasured. A 20 GB mail volume is a placeholder until the JMAP quota read gives a byte count.

Immich serves about 5 light users. Immich's requirements are 6 GB of RAM minimum and 8 GB recommended, with 2 cores minimum and 4 recommended. A 4 GB host can run Immich only with machine learning turned off. The database should sit on local SSD and never on a network share, and thumbnails and transcodes add 10 to 20% to the library ([Immich requirements](https://docs.immich.app/install/requirements/)). The library is about 19.3 GB, so photos, derivatives, and the database come to roughly 25 GB.

dubplate serves about 10 users. It never transcodes, because it serves the stored FLAC untouched (dubplate `BACKLOG.md`), so its CPU cost is small and its cost is network. The music library is 6.6 GB.

For traffic, take the owner's figure of about 1 Mbps for a lossless stream. One stream for one hour moves about 0.45 GB. A typical month of three concurrent streams for six hours a day is about 240 GB out. A heavy month of five concurrent streams for twelve hours a day is about 810 GB out. Immich browsing adds tens of GB. Restic pulls to pavilion add one full copy of about 35 GB at the start, then small increments. Mobile uploads are inbound, which every provider below either does not count or does not charge for. The worst case is about 1 TB out a month at a peak rate of 5 to 10 Mbps. No plan below has a port too slow for that.

The one-server shape fits in 8 GB only with limits. Immich wants the full 8 GB on its own. Stalwart needs well under 1 GB and dubplate needs little. So the Compose file has to cap Immich ML memory, as the owner proposed, and give Stalwart a CPU and memory reservation so an ML burst cannot starve mail. The two-server shape needs no caps: the mail box needs 2 GB, and the media box needs 8 GB to keep ML on.

Docker host networking is a feature of Docker Engine on Linux. The container shares the host's network namespace, and published-port options are ignored ([Docker host network driver](https://docs.docker.com/engine/network/drivers/host/)). Every provider below sells KVM virtual machines, so host networking works the same at all of them. No provider below documents a block on inbound port 25. The one exception to check is OVHcloud Local Zones, which "cannot contact SMTP servers under any circumstances" ([OVHcloud VPS FAQ](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/bare-metal-cloud/virtual-private-servers/vps-faq)). Never order an `LZ` plan for Stalwart.

The block volume's job is to stop a full disk from stopping mail. A separate filesystem does that job, whether it sits on a block device or a partition. A real volume also survives a rebuild of the OS disk, which a partition does not.

## OVHcloud (US Hillsboro, US Vint Hill, Canada Beauharnois)

OVHcloud's new "VPS 2027" line is cheaper than its 2026 line. The US catalog lists VPS-1 2027 (2 vCores, 4 GB, 40 GB NVMe, 500 Mbps unmetered) at $5.35 a month with no commitment. VPS-2 2027 (4 vCores, 8 GB, 75 GB NVMe, 1 Gbps) is $10.00, and VPS-3 2027 (6 vCores, 12 GB, 100 GB NVMe, 2 Gbps) is $14.50. A twelve-month upfront term drops them to $4.54, $8.50, and $12.32. The US plans are orderable in `US-WEST-OR` (Hillsboro) and `US-EAST-VA` (Vint Hill), and the Canadian catalog lists the same prices for BHS. Each plan includes one IPv4 address, one IPv6 /128, unlimited traffic, and a 99.9% SLA in the catalog data. The marketing page shows the upfront prices as "starting at" figures ([OVHcloud US VPS](https://us.ovhcloud.com/vps/)).

Debian 13 is in the OS list for every 2027 plan. The catalog's add-ons include a snapshot option ($0.40 a month on VPS-1, $0.60 on VPS-2), a Premium seven-day backup ($1.40 and $2.20), and additional disks from 50 GB at $2.60 to 100 GB at $5.20. The catalog does not say whether the additional disk is local or network storage. The catalog also lists a mandatory "Automated Backup Standard" line at $0.50 or $0.60, while the [VPS FAQ](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/bare-metal-cloud/virtual-private-servers/vps-faq) says the daily backup is included free. Budget up to $0.60 extra per server until a first invoice settles it.

The mail policy is the friendliest among the providers I checked. The FAQ says "Port 25 is not blocked by default on OVHcloud VPS servers," except in Local Zones. OVHcloud watches outbound SMTP with Vade Secure. A spam detection blocks port 25 at the network level for an hour in the API example, and repeat blocks last longer ([OVHcloud AntiSpam guide](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/bare-metal-cloud/dedicated-servers/antispam-best-practices)). PTR is set per IP through the API or the Terraform `ovh_ip_reverse` resource ([provider docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ovh/terraform-provider-ovh/master/docs/resources/ip_reverse.md)). Toward Microsoft, OVH ranges have the same trouble as Hetzner's. 2026 community threads report Outlook.com S3150 refusals, and an older thread reports UCEPROTECT level 3 listings ([OVH community: Blacklist outlook](https://community.ovhcloud.com/t/blacklist-outlook/24408), [UCEPROTECT thread](https://community.ovhcloud.com/t/blacklisted-ip-ranges-by-uceprotectl3/22715)). With the relay, that affects only the direct-send fallback.

For provision from code, the official `ovhcloud` CLI (v0.15.0, released 2026-09-16) has `ovhcloud vps reinstall` with an image ID, an SSH key, and `--wait` ([CLI reference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ovh/ovhcloud-cli/main/doc/ovhcloud_vps_reinstall.md)). The API's `POST /vps/{serviceName}/rebuild` takes a `postInstallScript`, which can stand in for a small cloud-init file. That endpoint and the snapshot endpoints are marked BETA in the US API schema. Ordering is a purchase, not a create call. The Terraform `ovh_vps` resource places an order on create, runs a termination on destroy, and can import an existing VPS ([provider docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ovh/terraform-provider-ovh/master/docs/resources/vps.md)). The practical shape is to order once, then script rebuilds, rDNS, and firewall rules.

The Edge Network Firewall is stateless, allows 20 rules per IP, and cannot open a port the host blocks ([Edge Network Firewall guide](https://docs.ovhcloud.com/en/guides/bare-metal-cloud/dedicated-servers/firewall-network.md)). At OVH, nftables on the host must be the enforced firewall. That fits host networking well: with no Docker NAT for Stalwart, the host rules apply directly.

A 24-hour VPSBenchmarks run on a US VPS-2 2027 in June 2026 found an "Intel Core Processor (Haswell, no TSX)" with 0.02% maximum steal and a 2% throughput variation ([VPSBenchmarks, June 2026](https://www.vpsbenchmarks.com/trials/ovhcloud_us_performance_trial_20Jun2026/endurance_runs)). Haswell meets Immich's x86-64-v2 floor. Per core it is likely slower than newer chips. Disk and network were not tested. A status aggregator lists a Hillsboro network and bare-metal outage of about 1 hour 51 minutes on 2026-02-20, and a roughly 13-hour US VPS incident in August 2026 during which reboots and reinstalls were slow ([StatusGator](https://statusgator.com/services/ovhcloud/outage-history), [IsDown](https://isdown.app/status/ovhcloud-us/incidents/644453-issue-affecting-vps-service)). These are third-party records with some date inconsistencies. Signup can stall on identity documents: one community report describes an order "Awaiting Documents" for 11 days ([OVH community](https://community.ovhcloud.com/t/order-177027041-in-awaiting-documents-status-for-11-days/11440)). I found no 2026 pattern of US VPS accounts being terminated.

Nothing binds at OVH for this workload. Traffic is unlimited, the ports are 500 Mbps and 1 Gbps, VPS-1 has twice the RAM the mail load needs, and VPS-2 meets Immich's recommended 8 GB and 4 cores.

## Hetzner Cloud

A Cloud API read on 2026-10-17 showed CX23 (2 vCPU, 4 GB, 40 GB) at $6.49, orderable in nbg1 and hel1 but not fsn1. CX33 (4 vCPU, 8 GB, $9.99) was sold out in every EU location. CAX21 (Arm, 4 vCPU, 8 GB, 80 GB, $12.49) was orderable in fsn1 only, and CAX11 (Arm, 4 GB, $6.99) everywhere. The baseline brief saw CX23 in hel1 only a few hours earlier, so stock moves within hours. If CX33 returns, the one-server shape drops to $12.12. IPv4 is $0.60. Volumes are $0.0767/GB with a 10 GB minimum and survive a rebuild ([Volumes](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/volumes/overview/)). EU types include 20 TB of traffic and charge $1.20 per extra TB. The US types in hil and ash include only 2 to 3 TB at three to six times the price.

Immich supports arm64, so CAX21 works for media. VPSBenchmarks found Intel Skylake and 0.00% steal on CX23 in August 2026 ([VPSBenchmarks CX23](https://www.vpsbenchmarks.com/trials/hetzner_performance_trial_28Aug2026/endurance_runs)). The API and `hcloud` CLI are the most mature here, the Cloud Firewall is stateful, and the account and dubplate's scripts already exist. Port 25 outbound needs a limit request, and the Microsoft reputation history is in the mail brief. The decisive cost is latency: 203 ms or more from Anchorage on every media request. Nothing binds on resources or traffic.

## netcup (EU and Manassas)

netcup's G12.5 VPS line is VPS 500 (2 vCores, 4 GB, 64 GB) at EUR 8.26 net and VPS 1000 (4 vCores, 8 GB, 128 GB) at EUR 14.50 net on a 12-month term. One-month terms cost more ([netcup VPS](https://www.netcup.com/en/server/vps)). Traffic is a flat rate, and IPv4 is included. Local Block Storage is listed at EUR 0.012 per GB, and a forum post reports a 21.52% rise from 2026-05-01 (unverified). Manassas is on the US East Coast, likely near OVH Vint Hill's 142 ms; I did not measure it. A location cannot be changed after setup, and prices are in euros.

I could not find netcup's own port 25 policy. A third-party page says new VPSs carry a "netcup Mail block" in the panel firewall that the customer removes ([smithery notes](https://smithery.ai/skills/dimdasci/netcup-vps-provider), unverified). netcup may temporarily deactivate products "in case of acute danger" and takes responses only through a panel field ([netcup abuse notices](https://www.netcup.com/en/helpcenter/documentation/security/abuse-notices)). A December 2025 review says every server on one account was deactivated after a single abuse report ([Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/netcup.com?page=7)). That is the account risk the owner wants to avoid. The REST API went stable on 2025-11-01, a community package claims it cannot create servers (unverified), and the Terraform providers are community projects for the retired SOAP service ([netcup REST API](https://www.netcup.com/en/helpcenter/documentation/server/rest-api), [rincedd provider](https://github.com/rincedd/terraform-provider-netcup-scp)). netcup costs more than OVH for the same RAM and sits farther from Alaska.

## BuyVM (Frantech)

BuyVM lists Slice 2048 (1 fair-share core, 2 GB, 40 GB) at $7 and Slice 4096 (1 dedicated core, 4 GB, 80 GB) at $15. Slabs cost $1.25 per 256 GB, five snapshots are free per location, and rDNS is self-service ([BuyVM slices](https://buyvm.net/kvm-dedicated-server-slices/)). ## Akamai Linode, Vultr, DigitalOcean

The three large US clouds have mature tooling and Pacific locations. They cost three to four times OVH for either shape. The Linode API lists Linode 2GB at $12, 4GB at $24, and 8GB at $48, volumes at $0.10/GB, Seattle and Fremont regions, and a `linode/debian13` image. Transfer is pooled and inbound is free ([Akamai transfer docs](https://techdocs.akamai.com/cloud-computing/docs/network-transfer-usage-and-costs)). Akamai restricts 25, 465, and 587 on newer accounts until support lifts them ([Akamai send email](https://techdocs.akamai.com/cloud-computing/docs/send-email)), which blocks the 587 relay by default.

The Vultr API lists vc2-1c-2gb at $10, vc2-2c-4gb at $20, and vc2-4c-8gb at $40 in Seattle, Los Angeles, and San Jose, with Debian 13 available. Vultr blocks only outbound 25 and reviews unblock tickets case by case ([Vultr SMTP](https://docs.vultr.com/support/products/compute/why-is-smtp-blocked)). Overage is $0.01/GB ([Vultr overage](https://docs.vultr.com/support/platform/billing/what-is-the-bandwidth-overage-rate)). DigitalOcean's Basic Droplets are $24 for 4 GiB and $48 for 8 GiB, volumes are $0.10/GiB, and overage is $0.01/GiB ([DO pricing](https://www.digitalocean.com/pricing/droplets), [DO volumes](https://www.digitalocean.com/pricing/volumes), [DO bandwidth](https://docs.digitalocean.com/platform/billing/bandwidth/)). DigitalOcean blocks 25, 465, and 587 with no stated unblock path ([DO SMTP](https://docs.digitalocean.com/support/why-is-smtp-blocked/)). None of the three would hit a traffic limit. None is worth the price here.

## Oracle Cloud Always Free

Oracle cut the Always Free Ampere A1 allowance from 4 OCPUs and 24 GB to 2 OCPUs and 12 GB on 2026-06-15. It changed only its documentation, and free-only instances above the new limit were stopped until resized ([InfoQ, July 2026](https://infoq.com/news/2026/07/oracle-cloud-free-tier-limits/)). Idle instances can be reclaimed. Tenancies created after June 2021 cannot send on port 25 without a limits request ([OCI release note](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/releasenotes/changes/f7e95770-9844-43db-916c-6ccbaf2cfe24)). PTR records need a service request ([OCI reverse DNS](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Network/Concepts/reverse_dns.htm)), and whether a free account can file one is unverified. An unannounced halving of the allowance is exactly the stability risk the owner ruled out, so Oracle is out for both shapes.

RackNerd has Seattle and Los Angeles sites and reportedly leaves port 25 open. ## Recommendation

OVHcloud in Hillsboro wins both shapes. It is the cheapest orderable option in each, it is 53 ms from Anchorage against 203 ms for Hetzner EU, its traffic is unlimited, and it is the only provider here whose own docs say outbound port 25 is open by default. Its weak points are a stateless edge firewall, BETA rebuild endpoints, and Haswell-era CPUs. None of these is a blocker. Each one moves work into the repo: host nftables, a post-install script, and patience on the first ML index.

**One-server shape: OVHcloud VPS-2 2027 (4 vCores, 8 GB, 75 GB NVMe) in US-WEST-OR, plus a 50 GB additional disk for mail data. $12.60 a month**, plus up to $0.60 if the standard backup line bills. The 75 GB OS disk holds about 25 GB of Immich data, 6.6 GB of music, and Docker images. The 50 GB disk is the smallest OVH sells and gives the mail store its own filesystem. RAM is the limit that binds: Immich alone wants 8 GB, so ML needs a hard memory cap and Stalwart needs a reservation. For no caps and more headroom, VPS-3 2027 (6 vCores, 12 GB, 100 GB) plus the disk is $17.10. The runner-up is Hetzner CAX21 in fsn1 with a 20 GB volume at $14.62, which trades 150 ms of latency for the more mature API and stateful firewall.

**Two-server shape: OVHcloud VPS-1 2027 (2 vCores, 4 GB, 40 GB) for mail and VPS-2 2027 (4 vCores, 8 GB, 75 GB) for media, both in US-WEST-OR. $15.35 a month**, plus up to $1.10 for the backup lines. Nothing binds: the mail box has four times Stalwart's stated need, and the media box meets Immich's recommendation with ML on and no caps. The runner-up is a split, with mail on Hetzner CX23 in nbg1 ($7.09) and media on OVH VPS-2 ($10.00), for $17.09. That puts the two servers in separate accounts, so a billing hold or abuse flag at one provider cannot take down both, and it keeps mail on the stateful Hetzner firewall. It costs a second provider in the repo and in the runbook. All Hetzner EU is third at $20.18.

**Which shape wins.** The one-server shape wins on cost, by $2.75 a month. The two-server shape wins on stability, and it is the shape I recommend. Mail is the account-recovery root, and on one box it shares a kernel, a Docker daemon, an unattended-upgrades reboot, a disk controller, and an IP address with Immich. Immich ships breaking releases often. A failed Immich upgrade, an ML out-of-memory kill, or a Docker daemon restart would then touch mail. On two boxes none of those can. Performance is close to a tie: one VPS-2 gives Immich and mail four shared vCores under caps, while two boxes give mail two vCores of its own and Immich the full 8 GB. Two servers edge it. At $2.75 a month, the isolation is cheap. The OVH twelve-month term would bring the two-server total to $13.04, below today's one-server no-commitment price.

## Risks

- Stock and prices drift. Hetzner's cost-optimized types go in and out of stock by location within hours, OVH's 2027 line is new, and both providers repriced in 2026. Scripts must read live availability and fail clearly, and the runbook should link price pages rather than copy numbers.
- The OVH firewall model is different. The Edge Network Firewall is stateless with 20 rules per IP, so host nftables carries the real policy. A bad nftables rule can lock out SSH, so the baseline's lockout guards (a second session, rescue mode in the runbook) apply.
- The OVH API is less mature. Rebuild and snapshot endpoints are BETA and ordering is a purchase, so each box's provisioning is part manual (the order) and part scripted (rebuild, rDNS, firewall).
- OVH appears to hold one snapshot per VPS. That is my reading of the single `/vps/{serviceName}/snapshot` resource. Take it before an Immich major upgrade and delete it after.
- OVH US hosts tested as Haswell. The first ML index of about 3,800 assets will run slower than on newer cores. It runs once.
- A new OVH account may sit in document review for days. Open it before the media rehearsal is scheduled.
- Outbound reputation matters if the relay fails. Hetzner and OVH ranges both have documented Microsoft trouble, so the mail brief's IP check before DNS still applies.
- Mailbox size is unmeasured. With 4 to 5 users, the 40 GB VPS-1 disk or the 50 GB mail disk may not be enough. Read the JMAP quota before ordering.
- The latency figures are one congested sample. A one-hour VPS-1 in Hillsboro (about one cent) would confirm how Immich and dubplate feel before committing.
