### Planning guidance carried from pass-core

A simple feature doesn't need extra configurability. ... - Defensive coding: Don't add error handling, fallbacks, or validation for scenarios that can't happen. Trust internal code and framework guarantees. Only validate at system boundaries (user input, external APIs). - Abstractions: Don't create helpers, utilities, or abstractions for one-time operations. Don't design for hypothetical future requirements. The right amount of complexity is the minimum needed for the current task."

Finish the whole task, and stop short of actions that are clearly beyond what was asked."

- **T1. The servers store (140 min, `auth-data`, pair with T2).**
  - *Outcome*: a servers-only age store committed encrypted, holding each secret in the form the services hold today; its writer; and the loader moved onto it.
  - *Files*: `secrets/servers.age`; `secrets/recipients.txt` (Geoff's workstation public key, the `RECIP` in the workstation `secret-set.sh:42`; his own line arrives at the sitting); `scripts/store.sh`; `scripts/lib/secrets.sh`; `secrets/registry.md`; `.gitattributes` (`secrets/servers.age binary`); `.gitignore` (`secrets/*.tmp`); `tests/harness/lib.sh`; `tests/store.test.sh`; `tests/secrets-lib.test.sh`.
  - *`store.sh`*: `set NAME` takes the value on stdin, never argv. `set-hash NAME --sha512-crypt|--bcrypt [--user U]` also reads the plaintext on stdin, but stores only its hash. The tools are in the Bluefin base image (`rpm -qf`: `openssl-3.5.9`, `httpd-tools-2.4.69`) and in Mint's apt (`openssl`, `apache2-utils`): `openssl passwd -6 -stdin` and `htpasswd -niB -C 10 U`. Probed: `-niB` reads stdin and prints `U:$2y$10$...` followed by one blank line, which `store.sh` strips; cost 10 is Go's `bcrypt.DefaultCost` (`golang.org/x/crypto/bcrypt/bcrypt.go:24`). `--bcrypt` refuses input over 72 bytes, since Go's `GenerateFromPassword` returns `ErrPasswordTooLong` past 72 (`bcrypt.go:95-97`) and htpasswd silently ignores the rest. Its other verbs are `delete NAME`, `names` (names only), and `rekey`, which re-encrypts to the recipients file.
