# Bulwark webmail: plan

## Brief

This plan builds the approved design `self-hosting/specs/2026-10-21-bulwark-design.md` (initiatives#114, `38d5b27`): Bulwark on orpheus at `webmail.907.life`, signing in to ward, themed by Survey's theme-limited recipe, with its upkeep and upgrades in the runbook. One servers branch and one workstation branch, whose PRs come back to Geoff in one packet through `conductor-servers`. The planned path is 525 minutes against the design's 300, under the 600-minute line where the standing answer applies. Two rulings below need Geoff at plan approval; nothing else needs him before launch. The three plan reviews, their fold, and its verification read (`self-hosting/research/2026-10-23-bulwark-plan-review-fold.md`) rebuilt the theme's output form, the live checks, and the back-out.

The pass runs in two phases (Geoff, 2026-10-23, in chat: start before `servers-backlog-2` merges, coordinating with the other agent; `conductor-servers` agreed the conditions below). Phase A starts at launch, alongside `servers-backlog-2`, and makes no live write. Phase B starts only on `conductor-servers`' "applies done" message, sent after backlog-2 (draft servers#14) has merged and the conductor's after-merge applies of orpheus and ward have run; a merge alone is not the signal. Phase A is T1 to T6 and T9 (290 minutes); phase B is T7, T8, and T10 (235 minutes). The wait between them is not on the path.

Eight facts found at planning or review correct the design's mechanisms without changing its outcome, and each goes in the servers PR's `## Decisions` and `docs/decisions.md`:

1. **CORS.** Stalwart has no per-origin CORS. Its one switch, `Http.usePermissiveCors`, sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` with the fixed allow-headers `Authorization, Content-Type, Accept, X-Requested-With`, a method list, and no `Access-Control-Allow-Credentials` (Stalwart v0.16.27, `crates/common/src/config/network.rs:404-427`). Ward sends no CORS header today: preflights from `https://webmail.907.life` to `/jmap/session` and `/.well-known/jmap` answered 204 with none (probed 2026-10-23). Bulwark's browser code calls the JMAP server directly (`lib/jmap/client.ts:1397`), so the switch must go on. The Fetch standard does not expose a response to a credentialed request when the allowed origin is `*`, and a request carrying `Authorization` stops at its preflight unless that header is allowed, so another site cannot read ward's answers with a visitor's credentials. What the switch does open is ruling 2: any page can now send wrong passwords to ward from a household member's browser.
2. **Auto-ban.** Bulwark's sign-in routes check each password from orpheus (`app/api/auth/session/route.ts:154`, `resolveJmapIdentity`), so the ban risk the design names is real. Stalwart's forwarded-header trust is global: with `Http.useXForwarded` on, it reads `Forwarded` and `X-Forwarded-For` from every connection (`crates/http/src/request.rs:749-775`), so turning it on would let any client choose its own address. The plan instead adds Stalwart `AllowedIp` objects for orpheus's IPv4 and IPv6 addresses. An allowed address skips the per-address and the per-login failure count (`is_auth_fail2banned`, `crates/common/src/network/security.rs:298-309`), the recipient, scanner, and loiter bans (`:218-290`), and the authenticated HTTP rate limit (`crates/common/src/auth/rate_limit.rs:22`), for every process on orpheus. This is ruling 1.
3. **The throttle.** Stock Caddy has no rate limiter (that is the third-party `caddy-ratelimit` module), so the design's Caddy throttle is not built. Bulwark budgets failed passwords per client address, keyed on the address Caddy forwards, so Bulwark trusts exactly one proxy hop (`TRUSTED_PROXY_DEPTH`, default 1, `lib/admin/session.ts:166`). The sign-in form's pre-check, `/api/auth/verify`, allows 5 per address and 30 across all clients per 15 minutes, and over budget it hands the check to the browser, which asks ward from the member's own address (`lib/auth/verify-budget.ts:16-23`; `app/api/auth/verify/route.ts:83-87`). The sign-in routes allow 5 more per address per 15 minutes, with no ceiling across clients (`verify-budget.ts:113-117`). So one address gets 10 tries through orpheus per 15 minutes. Under ruling 1 these are the only failure limits on the webmail path. Stalwart's own floor on a new password stays at its default, zxcvbn score 3 and 8 characters (`crates/registry/src/schema/structs_impl.rs:3428-3430`).
4. **The record type.** `webmail` is a CNAME to `orpheus.907.life`, like `dav` (`cloudflare/dns/orpheus`), in place of the design's A and AAAA pair.
5. **The secret's owner.** The design says "a root-only file"; the container runs as uid 1001, `nextjs` (Bulwark `Dockerfile:51, 56`), so the file is 0400 owned by uid 1001. On the host, uid 1001 is the next free account id (`roles/cloudflared/operators.sh:70` creates operators with a bare `useradd`), so the role reserves it as a system user `bulwark` before any operator can take it.
6. **The theme's form, and the first visit.** Bulwark reads an installed theme from three files in its config directory: `themes/registry.json`, `themes/<id>.css` holding compiled `:root` and `.dark` variables, and `themes/<id>.skin.css` (`lib/admin/plugin-registry.ts:358-366, 457-494`). A `manifest.json` is read only from a ZIP uploaded through the admin API, which is off, so the build writes the installed form directly. A browser gets the theme only from `/api/plugins` after a sign-in (`components/mail/mail-app.tsx:1284-1287`; the route needs a session, `app/api/plugins/route.ts:16-19`), and the first sync installs it without applying it (`stores/theme-store.ts:382-414`). The policy default then applies it from the next page load (`:140-170`). So a browser's first sign-in page, and the page right after its first sign-in, show stock Bulwark. That goes under the design's standing answer for a hook that cannot take a Survey value, and into the drafted upstream issue.
7. **Survey's second accent and the mark.** Survey's `--color-secondary` and `--color-accent` have no accent hook in Bulwark: Bulwark's `secondary` and `accent` are shadcn's quiet grounds, and its `chart-1` to `chart-5` are defined but drawn by no component (`app/globals.css:71-75`; no `components/` or `app/` file uses them). So they go under the same standing answer and into the upstream issue. The marks take Survey's own color for the benchmark triangle, `--site-contour` (`theme/components.css:68-73`).
8. **Outbound defaults.** The design keeps telemetry and settings sync off. Three more defaults leave the estate, and the plan closes them in the same posture. The hourly update check to `version.telemetry.bulwarkmail.org` (`instrumentation.node.ts:66-71`) goes off, since the monthly review carries upgrades. `/api/translate`, which sends message text to the public MyMemory API (`.env.example:455-461`), answers 404 at Caddy. `/api/favicon`, which fetches each correspondent's domain icon from `icons.duckduckgo.com` (`app/api/favicon/route.ts:478-479`; `senderFavicons` defaults on, `stores/settings-store.ts:913`), answers 404 at Caddy too, and the avatar falls back to initials (`components/ui/avatar.tsx:237-278`). Web push stays, since it registers only after a member grants the browser's permission (`components/push-notification-prompt.tsx:294`). Bulwark also remembers every sign-in for 30 days, with the mailbox password encrypted inside the cookie, unless the sign-in page offers a box (`.env.example:342-344`, `lib/auth/session-cookie.ts:2`), so the page offers it, unticked (`app/(main)/[locale]/login/page.tsx:204`).

The advisory watch the design names (Pass 6's C6) is not built yet. This plan amends C6's line in the base plan to watch `bulwarkmail/webmail` too, and T9 adds it to the watch list if C6 has merged first.

## Rulings needed

Each is a yes-or-no question for the plan's packet. The tasks build the recommended answer, and the other answer's changes are listed with it.

1. **Keep the orpheus allowlist, with Bulwark's per-address budgets as the webmail path's only failure limits?** Recommended: yes. Without it, about 100 wrong passwords a day through orpheus, from any mix of clients, ban orpheus for 24 hours (Stalwart's default `authBanRate`, `structs_impl.rs:35589-35592`; `authBanPeriod` 86400000 in `roles/stalwart/plan.ndjson:6`), which takes webmail away from both users and blocks orpheus's own submission on port 587; the sign-in routes have no ceiling across clients to stop that (fact 3). With it, a guesser spread over many addresses gets 10 tries per address per 15 minutes against any mailbox, the admin account included, with no account-level stop, and every service on orpheus inherits ban-free access to ward. The mailbox passwords are generated (base plan L3) and the admin password is 32 random characters, so the recommendation rests on them. Yes builds T4's `AllowedIp` line, T7 step 10, and T9's amendment of `docs/decisions.md:109`, which says the auto-ban covers the admin account. No removes every `AllowedIp` mention: T4's line and its test; the `AllowedIp` read-back in T7 step 4 and all of T7 step 10; the ban-list standing answer's "trace why the allowlist missed"; the back-out's `destroy` lines; fact 2's last two sentences; and T9's allowlist text and its `:109` amendment. The runbook's unban step then covers orpheus.
2. **Accept that the CORS switch lets any web page a household member opens spend that member's address's ban allowance?** Recommended: yes. After the switch, a hostile page can send wrong-password JMAP requests from the member's browser, and 100 a day ban that address for 24 hours on every protocol, locking out every device behind it (risk review R3; the review found no path that does this today). It needs a page aimed at this household, and the unban is on the emergency screens. Yes records it beside fact 1 and adds this cause to the runbook's "Unban a client from Stalwart's auto-ban". No also sets `authBanPeriod` to 3600000 (one hour) in `roles/stalwart/plan.ndjson:6` in T4, which shortens the ban for every client, attackers included.
