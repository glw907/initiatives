# Club sites survey, 2026-11-14

On 2026-11-14 four background research agents read the public front pages of 51 club, team, and
league websites, plus 17 site templates and published guidance sources, for the Pennant club theme
round. They used public pages only, read-only, with no sign-in and no forms; the pages were read as
text through a page-to-text fetcher, so colors, fonts, and photo content were mostly not seen.

site reports total 16 + 17 + 18 = 51 sites. The cross-report sums below are my addition of the

## Open points

- R1 to R3 do not use one element list or one definition. R1 counts "Register or join call to
  action in the body or an announcement bar", R2 "Join or membership call to action in body or
  header button", R3 "Join, register or tryout call to action on the page". The 40 of 51 join
  total adds three differently defined counts.
- The user brief called the reports "youth (16)", "competitive or adult club teams (about 18)",
  and "a third group". The reports themselves are youth clubs and leagues (R1, 16), member-run
  community clubs (R2, 17), and club teams (R3, 18, with 5 school teams excluded from the 18).
  16 + 17 + 18 = 51 reproduces the total only when R2 is counted at 17.
- R4 says its section tally "covers the 9 entries with section-level data" but names entries
  1, 2, 3, 4 (counted as two), 12, 14, and 15, which is 8. The "5 of 9" fixtures figure uses
  the 9, so its denominator cannot be reproduced from the report.
- R4 says entries 8 and 9 are sales pages, and its team-color tally lists Soccer Club in both
  the picker group and the preset group, so the groups overlap.
- R1 gives no tally for fixtures or results; R2 gives no
  tally for program cards (its Hawthorn and Foothills entries mention them). Sums over these
  elements therefore have a smaller denominator, marked above.
- R2 counts news as "5 (6 counting Sanctuary's 2024 stories)"; the 22 of 51 news sum uses 5.
- R3's contact count is "about 12", and R2 gives "about 14" for a hero statement and "roughly 8"
  for join prompts above the fold; these are the reports' own approximations.
- R1 lists Google Sites and similar sites without a URL (sites 5, 10, 13); no URL was recorded
  for them in the report.
- R4 notes it read only vendor descriptions for all templates except the Themestate demo, so its
  "5 of 9" is what vendors say their home pages carry, not rendered demos.

#### Platform versus general-builder sites
- **Platform sites** (SportsEngine, Crossbar, SportsPilot, SkiClubPro, Nordic Pulse; sites 1, 2, 4,
  6, 8, 9, 14, 16): more utility chrome (Login, Sign Up, Register, Shop), deeper nav, news lists,
  schedule tickers and sponsor strips come as templates. Their failure mode is template leftovers
  (lorem ipsum, "BUTTON", dead "#", duplicate blocks) and a feed that nobody curates. The two
  NCCYHA-style SportsEngine pages are text-and-banner with no photos.
- **General builder sites** (Google Sites, Wix, Weebly, WordPress; sites 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13):
  free-form, and the owner chose the order. They lead with one announcement (JUSC), a mission line
  (MCLL), or photos (Troop 599, SCRC). They lack scores and standings but link out to a portal for
  them (MCLL to TeamLinkt). Their failure modes are image-only sections with no alt text or text,
  undated posts, and stale footers.

#### Observations for a theme designer
1. **Make the registration call to action the one slot with an owner-set date.** Novi's hero
   ("2026-2027 REGISTRATION NOW OPEN" with a button), JUSC's bar and SCRC's closer all work; MCLL's
   "Spring Registration Opens in Late November" beside a Fall 2026 nav entry, and the Devils' dead
   Register link, show how it rots. Give it an expiry or an "opens on" date field.
2. **Design for empty states.** Empty event lists (NSAA "UPCOMING DATES", Stoughton Events calendar)
   are the most visible neglect. A block with no content should collapse, not show a heading.
3. **Never let a feed be the only content.** Novi, Jr. Sun Devils and Devils rely on tickers and
   news lists that age in public. Show dates on every item and de-emphasize anything past a
   threshold.
4. **Plan photo-light.** Most sites had no real photos (AHSTC reuses the logo; Stoughton, NCCYHA,
   Jr. Sun Devils have none). Troop 599 and Blackhawk show kids in gallery or news photos with no
   visible captions naming children (inference from the text; I could not see images). A theme
   should look finished with logo and color only, and give photos an optional slot with no required
   child names.
5. **Seasonal quick facts earn their place.** Meeting day and place (Troop 599), fields, closures
   and directions (JUSC, Nelson, SCRC, NCCYHA) are the parent-facing items that were present and
   current. Make "Where and when" and "Know before you go" blocks first-class, ahead of sponsor
   strips.
6. **Cap the nav.** Blackhawk, FSYO, Devils and Troop 599 all run 8 to 12 top-level or deeply nested
   items with duplicates. The common set is About, Programs, Calendar or Schedule, Registration,
   Donate or Support, Contact, Store, Login.

##### Photo consent and safeguarding (affects the theme directly)

- The FA, Photographing and Filming Children, v1.3 (2025),
  https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/21-10-2025/83-photography-and-filming-children.ashx
  - Clubs must hold written parental consent before using a player's photo on the club website.
  - Do not publish names with photos without written consent.
  - Do not "use player profiles with pictures and detailed personal information online".
  - Clubs need a way to exclude non-consented children, and editing or blurring them out is allowed.
- Volleyball England, Use of Photography,
  https://volleyballengland.org/uploads/docs/Use_of_Photography.pdf
  - Do not supply full names with images, with exceptions such as elite players.
  - Use suitable kit only.
  - "Consider using models or illustrations" when promoting an activity.
  - Wide event shots are treated differently from close-ups.
- NSPCC CPSU, https://sport.nspcc.org.uk/resource-library/feature-boxes/key-info-photography/
  - "Do not publish a full name, fixture location, club name, travel plans or training time with an
    image."
  - Prefer images that "focus on the activity rather than the child".
  - In swimming and gymnastics, crop above the shoulders.
  - Illustrations can replace photos.
  - Ask the child even if the parent consents.
  - Withdrawing consent after publication is "very difficult to recall".
- Club-hosted USA Swimming and water polo policies appeared in search results only. They say parents
  may withhold consent, and that a minor's full name must not appear with school, home area, or
  schedule.

##### Where sources agree

1. The home page answers who you are, what you offer, and how to join (NN/g, USA Swimming, Sprocket
   snippet, Soccerscene).
2. One or a few top tasks sit above the fold, usually register or join, then schedule (NN/g,
   JerseyWatch, Soccerscene).
3. Fresh news or announcements sit on the home page (NN/g, Soccerscene, Little League, USA
   Swimming).
4. Contact and a footer of practical details are present (NN/g, Soccerscene, Sprocket).
5. Sponsors and partners appear (USA Swimming, Little League, Soccerscene).
6. Safeguarding bodies agree: consent, no full name beside an image, activity-focused framing.

##### Where templates and guidance disagree

- Templates lead with league data (fixtures, results, standings: 5 of 9 entries) and squad cards
  with numbers. Guidance for clubs leads with identity and joining. The FA advises against player
  profiles with pictures online.
- Hero carousels and animated headlines: USA Swimming recommends a carousel. NN/g says never to
  animate the headline, and Goal splits and animates its title
  (https://astro.build/themes/details/goal/).
- None of the templates showed a safeguarding link. USA Swimming recommends one.

##### Observations to act on

1. Make every photo slot optional and safe by default. Build squad and news cards so they work with
   a number, role, and first name or initials, and no portrait, per the FA and NSPCC. Provide an
   illustration or crest fallback (Volleyball England, NSPCC). Never render a full name beside an
   image, and keep fixture location and training time out of image captions (NSPCC).
2. Order the front page identity, then one primary join or register CTA, then next date or fixture,
   then news, then sponsors and footer practicalities. Sources: NN/g's tagline and "1–4 core tasks"
   (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/113-design-guidelines-homepage-usability/) and JerseyWatch's
   next-date priority.
3. Treat league tables and scoreboards as an optional block, not the default hero. Most guidance is
   for rec and youth clubs, where schedule and registration come first (Little League, JerseyWatch).
   The league-first layouts come from the SportsPress-style themes (entries 1 to 4).
4. Build color on a small token set: primary and accent plus a logo slot, with a default palette the
   club overrides in one place. Arena and Goal do this with CSS custom properties, GOAT with one
   accent, and USA Swimming asks for one consistent palette. Contrast on team colors is an inference
   to check, supported by JerseyWatch's low-contrast warning.
5. Signal "team" with crests or badges, numerals, scoreboard-style type, and a countdown rather than
   with photos. These devices recur in entries 1 and 2 and need no images of children. Do not claim
   stripes or diagonals as conventions. I found none.
6. Add a Safe Sport or safeguarding link and a privacy link to the footer (USA Swimming's Safe Sport
   tab; NN/g's privacy link guideline).
